# Image to Video with FreeVideo AI: Animate a Product Photo or Portrait

Image to Video starts from a still image and uses a written prompt to guide movement. It is useful for a subtle product reveal, a portrait animation or a small camera move across an illustration. Each generation costs 8 credits and produces a silent MP4.

[Open Image to Video](https://freevideoai.app/ai-video-generator?mode=image-to-video)

## Prepare the image first

Use a clear image with a single main subject and enough space around it for the intended camera movement. Upload a **PNG, JPEG or WebP file no larger than 10 MB**.

Choose a source composition close to your intended video orientation. Video output offers landscape 16:9 and portrait 9:16. A square or 3:4 source can be used, but the final video framing may differ. If the edges matter, prepare a matching composition in an editor first.

For a product, check that its outline is easy to distinguish from the background. For a portrait, use an image with a clear face and avoid heavy occlusion. Use images you have permission to use.

## Generate the clip

1. Sign in and choose **Image to Video**.
2. Upload the starting image and confirm that the preview is the intended file.
3. Choose 5, 10 or 15 seconds and the video orientation.
4. Describe **what should move**, rather than redescribing every visible detail.
5. Start generation and watch the task status.
6. Preview the completed clip, inspect the full frame, then download the MP4.

Start with a short, restrained movement. Strong rotations and large viewpoint changes require the model to invent unseen surfaces.

## Product example: preserve the subject, move the camera gently

```text
The product remains upright and centered. The camera makes a very slow straight push-in. Soft reflections move subtly across the glass surface while the background stays calm. Keep the overall product silhouette consistent in one continuous shot.
```

This requests preservation; it does not guarantee exact packaging, printed text or geometry. Examine label edges, cap shape and reflections before publishing. If branding must be exact, use a workflow that preserves the original product layer and add motion in a conventional editor.

## Portrait example: small natural movement

```text
The person makes a subtle natural head movement and blinks gently. The camera remains still. Keep the original framing and soft lighting, with a quiet background and restrained movement throughout the shot.
```

Begin with slight movement rather than an extreme expression change. Check eyes, teeth, hands and face shape in the result. This public workflow does not provide an audio or lip-sync control.

## A practical review checklist

- Is the product or person recognizable throughout the clip?
- Do important edges stay within the frame?
- Does movement match the prompt?
- Have labels, fingers, reflections or background objects changed unexpectedly?
- Is the video suitable for its intended use after any editing?

A good first frame does not guarantee a consistent final frame. Review the entire clip, including the beginning and end.

## Troubleshooting

| Problem | Next step |
| --- | --- |
| Upload is rejected | Check PNG/JPEG/WebP format and the 10 MB limit; re-export if necessary |
| The subject changes too much | Reduce motion, remove rotation and request a fixed camera |
| The image becomes overly cropped | Use a source closer to 16:9 or 9:16 and leave more space around the subject |
| The label is unreadable | Use simpler motion; do not rely on generation to reproduce typography |
| The task is still processing | Wait for a final status before submitting another version |

If you need to guide both endpoints, use [First & Last Frame](https://freevideoai.app/ai-video-generator?mode=first-last-frame).
