# Getting Started with FreeVideo AI: Your First Clip, Credits & Downloads

FreeVideo AI turns text or still images into short, silent MP4 videos. This guide helps you choose a mode, understand the credit cost and finish a small first project without confusing image settings with video settings.

## 1. Pick a mode based on what you already have

| What you have | Choose | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A scene idea written in words | Text to Video | No starting photo is required |
| One photo or illustration | Image to Video | The image provides the visual starting point |
| Two images showing an intended beginning and ending | First & Last Frame | Both images guide the transition |

Open the [video generator](https://freevideoai.app/ai-video-generator). Sign in with the Google account you intend to use for your generation history. Select the mode before writing your prompt.

## 2. Check credits before starting

Text to Video costs 6 credits per generation. Image to Video and First & Last Frame cost 8 credits each. Eligible new Google accounts receive 60 welcome credits, which expire after 30 days. Eligibility matters: creating another account does not guarantee another grant.

For planning, 60 credits cover ten 6-credit Text to Video generations, or seven 8-credit image-based generations with 4 credits left, if the entire balance is available. These are cost calculations, not extra free-generation offers.

Paid packs are one-time purchases: US$6.99 for 100 credits and US$29.90 for 1,000 credits. Paid credits do not expire. Review [Pricing](https://freevideoai.app/pricing) for current purchase availability.

## 3. Make a simple first clip

1. Select **Text to Video**.
2. Choose **5 seconds** and **16:9** for a short landscape test.
3. Enter the prompt below.
4. Check your selected settings and start generation.
5. Follow the task status in your history. Queue time and processing time vary.
6. When the result is ready, preview it and download the MP4.

```text
A ceramic coffee cup sits on a wooden table beside a window. A thin ribbon of steam rises slowly. The camera makes a gentle push-in toward the cup. Soft morning light, a quiet background, one continuous shot.
```

The prompt gives the model one subject, one visible action and one camera movement. It avoids asking for several scene changes in a short clip.

## 4. Review before spending credits on another attempt

Watch the full clip once for the subject, once for movement and once for unwanted changes. If the camera moves too much, revise only the camera instruction. If the background distracts, simplify the setting. Changing one variable makes comparisons easier.

The public workflow outputs **silent MP4**. Add music, narration, captions or sound effects later in a video editor. A prompt asking someone to speak does not add a public lip-sync or audio-generation feature.

## Settings to know

Video offers 5, 10 or 15 seconds and landscape 16:9 or portrait 9:16. The workflow controls the output resolution; there is no public 4K export switch. Longer clips are not automatically more coherent, so use a short test to establish the look first.

H3 Image is a separate text-to-image tool. Its 4:3 and 3:4 options do not change which aspect ratios are available in the video generator.

## If something looks stuck

- **Queued or generating:** allow the active task to finish; avoid repeatedly submitting the same prompt.
- **Checking the result:** the service may still be reconciling the task. Review its final status before retrying.
- **History is missing:** check that you are using the same account, then refresh the history.
- **No sound:** this is expected for the current public video workflow.
- **Unexpected output:** simplify the request; generation is not an exact simulation or guaranteed reproduction.
